# Discovery Brief

Run before any strategy or visual work. Ask in batches of three or four, not all
at once. If the user can't answer something, note it as an assumption and flag it
in the final deliverable rather than quietly filling it in.

## Business

1. What does the company actually sell, in one sentence, without adjectives?
2. What stage — pre-launch, early revenue, established, rebrand?
3. If rebranding: what exists today, what's working, what's forcing the change?
   What equity must survive? (Existing recognition is an asset; discarding it has a cost.)
4. Budget and timeline for execution. This determines whether the mark can involve
   custom lettering or must work with a licensed typeface.

## Audience

5. Who is the primary audience? Push past demographics — what is their situation
   at the moment they encounter this brand?
6. What do they currently do instead? (The real competitor is often a spreadsheet,
   a habit, or nothing.)
7. What do they need to believe to act?
8. Secondary audiences: investors, recruits, press, partners. Which matter?

## Category

9. Name three to five competitors. What do their brands have in common?
   That shared language is the category convention — the thing to deliberately
   break or deliberately join.
10. Is the category crowded and legible, or new and unexplained? Crowded rewards
    distinctiveness; new rewards clarity.
11. Any category signals that are non-negotiable? (Fintech usually can't look
    unserious; healthcare usually can't look experimental.)

## Personality

12. Three adjectives you want. Then: three adjectives you'd refuse.
    The refusals are more informative than the wants.
13. Name a brand in any category you admire, and say specifically what about it.
14. Name a brand you'd hate to be mistaken for.

## Constraints

15. Where does this appear? Screen only, or print, packaging, signage, embroidery,
    vehicle wrap? Physical application constrains form far more than screen does.
16. Regulatory or compliance requirements affecting color, claims, or symbols.
17. Existing assets that stay — a typeface license, a color from a parent brand,
    a mark from an acquisition.
18. Who signs off? A single founder and a committee produce different processes.

## Output

Close discovery with a written summary the user confirms before you proceed:
audience, category position, personality, constraints, and an explicit list of
assumptions where answers were missing. Get agreement on this document. Every
later decision refers back to it.
